Overview

Code screens and guards. The AI committee judges. You approve.

A US$200k long-only paper fund in US large caps, held for months, with a hard maximum drawdown of 20%. Every trade passes through three different kinds of decision-maker, and each one can only do its own job.

Deterministic code AI committee (Claude) You Automatic risk actions (no approval)
Code

Measures and limits

Downloads data, ranks ~500 stocks, sets the market regime and exposure caps, enforces every hard limit, places orders.

AI committee

Judges

Eight roles read the research pack and the news, then pick names and weights from the top-40 list. It can be more cautious than the code, never less.

You

Approve

Nothing the committee proposes trades until you approve it. Only you can edit the risk limits in fh/config.py.

Automatic

Protects

Stops, the drawdown ladder and the crash sentinel sell without asking, because waiting for a human in a crash costs money.

The AI never sees an order ticket. It writes a proposal file; code checks it, you approve it, code executes it.

Step 2 · The quant screen

Every stock gets a score; only the top 40 can be bought

Each measure is a percentile rank against all other eligible stocks, so the score is relative: a stock gets in by beating ~460 others, not by being famous. Missing data counts as average.

Lottery-ticket penalty

The most volatile 10% of stocks lose 0.10 points, so a wild chart cannot ride momentum alone into the pool.

The shadow portfolio: the benchmark to beat

The top 15 names, equal-weighted, max 4 per sector, at the same exposure cap. It is tracked daily. If the committee cannot explain why its picks beat the shadow, it should hold the shadow's picks.

Step 4 · The committee

Eight roles, in a fixed order

The four analysts work in parallel and cannot see each other's reports, so they cannot anchor on one another. Every number must come from the pack or a cited source.

Macro strategist

Cycle phase, rates, inflation, upcoming events. Recommends a max exposure ≤ the code cap.

Sector analyst

Sector leadership, breadth, hidden themes that span sectors (e.g. "oil shock").

Fundamental analyst

Latest results, guidance, red flags, next earnings date. Rates Buy / Hold / Avoid.

Technical analyst

Trend strength and entry timing: buy now / wait / avoid. Flags very volatile names.

Decision logic · Buying

How a stock earns a place, and how big it gets

In the top 40 of the screen
Hard rule in code. Anything else is dropped.
▼
Fundamental: Buy, no unresolved red flag
A guidance cut or accounting issue overrides a great score.
▼
Technical: buy now (or "wait" at a starter weight)
Avoid chasing names that are stretched far above their trend.
▼
Sector favoured or neutral, theme budget left
Keeps the portfolio from being one macro bet in disguise.
▼
Survives the risk check and your approval

Sizing

  • 4%–10% per stock, more for higher conviction.
  • Starter weight (near 4%) when results are due within ~10 trading days; add after the numbers.
  • Smaller weights for very volatile names.
  • 10–15 names once more than 30% is invested.
  • ≤ 30% in any one sector (code scales it down if breached).
  • Staging: a new fund is usually deployed over 2–3 weeks, not at one price.

Example: first committee (16 Oct)

11 names, 54% invested although the code allowed 100%: a cluster of Fed, earnings and GDP events in the next two weeks. LLY and CVX were cut to smaller weights by the risk manager because both reported within 10 days.

Decision logic · Selling

Two ways a position is reduced

Committee decides, you approve
  • Thesis broken: a holding rated Avoid, or with a broken trend, must be exited unless the PM writes an explicit reason to keep it.
  • Fell out of the top 40: may be held or trimmed, but code refuses to add to it.
  • Better use of the money: only if the replacement is clearly better; one-way turnover is budgeted at 40% a month.
  • Drift: weight changes smaller than 2 percentage points are ignored, so small drifts do not generate trades.
  • De-risk: the CIO can cut exposure below the cap at any time.
Automatic, no approval
  • Stop from entry: close 15% below the average buy price → sold at the next open.
  • Trailing stop: once in profit, close 25% below its highest close → sold.
  • Rank exit: a holding that ranks worse than 60 on the screen for 2 weekly reviews in a row → sold at the next open. It catches fading names before a stop is hit.
  • Server-side stop: a standing order at IBKR 20% below entry, which works even if your Mac is off.
  • Drawdown ladder and crash sentinel: cut every position pro-rata (next slide).
  • Kill switch / hard floor: the fund halts; no buys until you restart it.
Order of execution each session: risk exits → deleveraging → your approved rebalance. Within a rebalance, sells go first so they fund the buys.

Drawdown control

The ladder and the crash sentinel

Drawdown is measured from the fund's high-water mark. The ladder stops at −15%, leaving a buffer before the 20% mandate for overnight gaps and slippage.

Drawdown ladder: maximum invested

100%
75%
50%
0% · halt
0%−8%−12%−15%−20% mandate

Applied automatically at the next session (and intraday by the sentinel). After a halt, the fund stays in cash until you restart it.

Shock

Market proxy down 3% in a day, or VIX ≥ 30 → new buys frozen for about 3 sessions, alert sent.

Crash

Proxy down 5% in a day or 8% over 3 days, or VIX ≥ 40 → exposure cut to 50% at once, every holding pro-rata.

Kill switch

Create a file named STOP_TRADING in the fund folder → fund halts, no new buys.

The sentinel has no AI in it on purpose: it must be fast, cheap and predictable. Pro-rata cutting avoids a panicked call about which stock is "weakest" mid-crash.

Why market orders

The paper account only gets 15-minute-delayed quotes, which cannot price a limit order reliably. Adaptive orders work against IBKR's live price.

If something does not fill

A rejected order is re-sent as a plain market order. Unfilled approved orders are retried for up to 3 sessions, then the committee sees the gap.

Two runs cannot collide

A lock file prevents overlapping runs, and an approval already traded today is never sent twice.

Your controls and the honest caveats

What you control, and what the system cannot promise

You control
  • Approve or reject each proposal (Approval tab or scripts/approve.py).
  • Every risk limit, only by editing fh/config.py; agents can read limits but never change them.
  • The kill switch (STOP_TRADING file).
  • Restarting the fund after a hard-floor halt.
  • Everything is traceable in the Audit trail and research/<date>/.
Caveats
  • The AI part cannot be backtested. Models have read the future; only the code (screen, regime, ladder, stops) was backtested.
  • Survivorship bias. Backtests use today's S&P 500 members, which flatters results.
  • Delayed data. 15-minute quotes on paper; stops are checked on closing prices.
  • Gaps. A stock can open far below its stop; the ladder keeps a 5-point buffer for this.
  • Paper trading. Fills on paper are kinder than in a live market.
